# Model 12: domestication versus biological appearance

## Claim from the debate

The discussion connects the Qur'anic "eight pairs" of livestock to the period
around the proposed Adam date and suggests that the appearance of these animals
around the early agricultural transition may support a special-creation scenario.

The four livestock categories relevant here are sheep, goats, cattle, and camels.

## Category distinction

The model separates:

- **domestication / management date**: when human control, breeding, or husbandry
  becomes archaeologically or genetically detectable;
- **biological lineage existence**: whether the wild progenitor or ancestral
  lineage existed before domestication.

These are not the same event.

## Evidence encoded

Approximate evidence envelopes:

- sheep: domestication about 10-11 ka; a 13 ka wild sheep paleogenome predates it;
- goats: domestication about 9.5-10.5 ka from bezoar; major domestic goat
  mitochondrial lineages diverged far earlier than domestication;
- taurine cattle: domestication about 9.5-10.5 ka from Southwest Asian aurochs;
  aurochs populations existed vastly earlier;
- dromedary camel: domestication only about 3-4.5 ka, with earlier wild
  dromedaries documented in Arabia.

## Computation

Model 12 asks whether domestication-date intervals overlap a 9-12 ka early
Neolithic window.

Three of the four do. Dromedary camel does not.

Under a deliberately simple uniform-within-evidence-interval calculation, the
probability that **all four** domestication dates lie in the 9-12 ka window is
therefore exactly zero because the camel interval has no overlap.

This is an interval-consistency result, not a posterior probability of creation.

The model also asks whether wild-lineage evidence predates an 11 ka biological
origin claim. All four categories have older wild-lineage evidence.

## Judgment on the claim

**The empirical premise "these livestock biologically appeared around 11 ka" is
not supported.** The evidence instead distinguishes an early-Neolithic
domestication transition for sheep, goats, and taurine cattle from the much older
existence of their wild progenitors. Dromedary domestication is thousands of
years later than the proposed 11 ka date.

Therefore, clustering some domestication dates near the Neolithic transition
cannot by itself be evidence that the animal species were specially created at
that time.

### What would need to change for the stronger claim

A special biological-appearance claim would require evidence that the relevant
animal lineage was absent before the proposed date and appeared without a
pre-existing wild progenitor. The ancient-DNA and zooarchaeological evidence
encoded here points in the opposite direction.
